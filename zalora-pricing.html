Zalora: stop the markdown

Using 2,633 product-week sales records, we built a demand model for a Zalora-branded dress and found a counter-intuitive answer: the early price cut was premature, and holding the price at S$49.90 clears the same stock at a much higher margin.

Step 1 · Demand model

What actually drives units sold?

We filtered the historical data to Zalora-branded products and ran a correlation analysis to avoid multicollinearity. Impressions moved almost perfectly with clicks (ρ = 0.93), so we dropped them. Cost, price band and stock were dropped as either consequences of demand or redundant. The final regression predicts units sold from price and product clicks (a signal of real customer intent), controlling for weeks since release.

Units sold = 12.02 − 0.151 × Price + 0.0011 × Clicks + 0.017 × Weeks since release

Step 2 · Optimise

Maximise profit, subject to stock

Holding clicks at their post-launch average (500 a week), we maximised weekly profit, (price − S$15.39 unit cost) × demand, subject to only 39 units being left in stock. Solving the first-order condition gives an optimal price of about S$49.50 in every week, rounded to the retail price point of S$49.90.

The insight. At S$34.90 the dress sells about 7–8 units a week and runs out sooner, but at a much thinner margin. At S$49.90 it sells about 5 a week and still sells out by week 11, earning about S$176–179 a week. With limited stock, the question isn't "how do we sell more?" but "how do we earn the most from what we have?"

Looking back

What I'd tighten

  • The model explains 15% of the variation (R² = 0.15). The price effect is reliable, but demand for a single product is noisy. I'd add seasonality, promotion flags and competitor prices, and test a log-log model to estimate elasticity directly.
  • Consistency: our write-up said weeks since release was dropped, while the final equation kept it. It has no real effect (p = 0.52), but the model and the text should match.
  • Validate before you trust: backtest the recommended price on held-out weeks, or run a limited A/B price test.
