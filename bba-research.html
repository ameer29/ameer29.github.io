BBA · Amity Global Business School · 2018 – 2021

BBA in Marketing: learning to ask customers

I chose a BBA because I wanted to understand how a business actually works, from the core. Marketing wasn't the plan on day one; it grew on me over the three years, and by my final year it was what I wanted to do.

The two projects below are where that showed. Each was an independent primary-research study that I ran end to end over about three months: I chose the topic, designed the questionnaire, collected responses, analysed them and wrote up the findings.

In 2026 I went back to the raw survey exports, recalculated every figure and added analysis I didn't know how to do at the time. Those additions are marked Revisited.

Project 1 · Year 2

What keeps people on Netflix? A consumer preference & attitude study

Type Independent primary researchSample 92 survey responsesTools Google Forms, ExcelGuide Prof. Shanti J

The question

Streaming in India was getting crowded. I wanted to know what actually drives people's preference for Netflix: content, price, experience, or habit. I also wanted to know who they would switch to.

What I did

  • Designed a 17-question survey covering usage, feature satisfaction, discovery and loyalty
  • Collected 92 responses, mostly students aged 15–30
  • Analysed the results and wrote a full research report

What I found

  • People stay for the catalogue, not the price. Content breadth and quality were rated highest, and pricing lowest, yet 46% were on the most expensive Premium plan.
  • Loyalty is real but not universal. 38% said they'd always choose Netflix; Amazon Prime was the clear fallback for the rest.
  • Discovery runs through the algorithm. 92% at least sometimes browse Netflix's recommendations before choosing what to watch.

Project 2 · Final-year dissertation

How Swiggy wins: a business model & customer study

Type Independent dissertation + presentationSample 93 survey responsesFrameworks Business Model CanvasGuide Prof. S. Karthikeyan

The question

Swiggy survived the 2014–15 food-delivery funding wave when most rivals didn't. I wanted to understand how its business model works, and whether customers choose it for the reasons the company competes on.

What I did

  • Mapped Swiggy's business model with the Business Model Canvas, including its funding and acquisition timeline from 2014 to 2020
  • Designed and ran a 20-question customer survey during the pandemic
  • Wrote the dissertation and presented the findings in a 12-slide deck
+48
Net Promoter Score (Revisited)
84%
say food quality is their top concern
70%
say app discounts shape their ordering most

The insight I missed in 2021. When asked directly, 84% named food quality as their top concern. But when people explained in their own words why they pick one app, food quality came last. Speed and discounts came first. Quality is the baseline every app has to meet; it isn't what wins the customer. Customer service was the one reason cited almost only in Swiggy's favour.

My recommendations at the time

  • Go public on NSE and BSE. Swiggy listed in November 2024.
  • Expand internationally. This hasn't happened. Swiggy grew by moving into new categories in India instead, with Instamart for groceries.
  • Keep logistics at the core of the model. The survey backed this up: speed and reliability are what customers notice.

Beyond the classroom · Summer 2019

Digital Marketing Training, Internshala

Format 6-week online trainingDates 1 June – 13 July 2019

In the summer after my first year, I took a structured course to learn how marketing works online, beyond the theory in class.

What it covered

Blogging & web analyticsSEOEmail marketingInbound & content marketingSearch, display & video adsMobile marketingSocial media, ORM & automation

Looking back

What I'd do differently today

  • Sampling: both samples were about 80% students reached through my own network. Useful for a youth segment, but I'd now label it as that rather than as "consumers".
  • Question design: a few questions nudged answers, such as "which do you prefer and why". I'd use neutral wording and fixed scales.
  • Analysis: I reported percentages. Today I'd segment the results, for example by age, frequency and plan, and test whether the differences are significant.
  • Data hygiene: the raw exports had duplicate submissions and free-text entries in the nationality field. Recalculating from that raw data changed several of my original figures.

What carried forward from these years: a habit of going to real people for data instead of assuming. I learn by doing, then keep improving the result.
