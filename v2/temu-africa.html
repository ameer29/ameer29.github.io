Strategy case study · NUS Business School · Semester 2, 2025/26

Can Temu win in Africa, not just on price?

Temu entered South Africa, Nigeria and Morocco in 2024 on ultra-low prices. We analysed whether that model can last in Africa, and designed an "Africa Operating System" to build an advantage beyond price.

Course BMS5120 Innovation & Strategies for Emerging MarketsFaculty Prof. Ishtiaq MahmoodTeam 7My section Key strategic & organisational challenges

The question

Cheap gets you in. Does it keep you in?

Temu grew fast in Africa, reaching 52% of online shoppers in Morocco. But African e-commerce is shaped by weak last-mile logistics, low trust in paying upfront, cash-dominant payments, and tightening regulation. Our report asked whether a model built for China-to-door shipping could turn early growth into a durable position.

My contribution

Three reasons the current model can't last

I owned the diagnosis at the centre of the report: why Temu's model doesn't fit Africa. I built it around three structural challenges, each backed by evidence.

1 · A value proposition competitors can copy

  • A value curve analysis showed Temu wins only on price and variety, and both are easy to replicate.
  • Jumia had already matched factory-gate prices by opening a sourcing office in Yiwu, China.
  • African shoppers care most about payment flexibility, trust and reliable delivery, which are exactly Temu's weak points.
  • Requiring payment upfront creates a trust barrier in cash-based markets.

2 · A delivery system built for the wrong market

  • Delivery takes 15–20 days from Temu, against 24–48 hours from Jumia in Lagos and Abuja.
  • 90.3% of South African reviewers on Hellopeter gave Temu one star (Dec 2025).
  • Many streets lack formal addresses, so a model that assumes a verifiable address breaks down.
  • Key insight: the real competitor isn't another app. It's informal retail, about 85% of retail in these markets.

3 · No capability to handle regulation

  • The model relied on regulatory arbitrage: VAT loopholes and gaps in data rules.
  • Nigeria's data protection regulator opened an investigation covering 12.7 million users (Feb 2026); South Africa's National Consumer Commission opened its own probe.
  • Jumia spent a decade building relationships with regulators. Temu has none in Africa.

The team's recommendation

"Africa OS": from cheapest to trusted and fairly priced

Using Kim & Mauborgne's Four Actions Framework (eliminate, reduce, raise, create), the team proposed:

Raise
Payment flexibility (cash on delivery, M-Pesa and other mobile money) and a catalogue curated for local needs
Create
A Merchant Partner Network that turns informal shops into pickup points and cash-on-delivery agents, paid a commission per parcel
Reduce
Aggressive discounting, replaced by prices set to each country's purchasing power
Eliminate
Prepaid-only checkout as the default, and a one-size-fits-all global catalogue

The report adds a 3-horizon roadmap, a risk mitigation plan (regulatory, logistics, fraud and competition) and success metrics: repeat purchase rate, delivery time, contribution margin per order and customer acquisition cost.

What I learned

  • Low price is what customers give up, not a benefit they receive. A strategy needs something that can't be copied.
  • In emerging markets, the competitor that matters is often informal and invisible in market-share data.
