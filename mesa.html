Mesa: cook together, eat wider

Our brief was "Youth, loneliness in the age of AI." Our answer is a live web app that puts newly arrived students in a kitchen with three people from three other countries, then at a shared table every week.

The live app

  1. Join with your university email. Mesa runs inside one university, so everyone at your table is someone you'll run into again.
  2. Get matched into a table of four passports. Four students from four countries, so nobody has the home advantage.
  3. Pick a dish. One from each of your countries, plus a few that anyone can manage.
  4. Saturday: cook together. A shopping list, jobs you can swap, and a recipe video.
  5. Wednesday: one table. Four kitchens bring four dishes. Halfway through comes "the round": each person names one thing they're grateful for and one thing that's hard.

This is the real app, embedded. It's interactive.

The problem

You just moved. Nobody knows you yet.

International students arrive in a new city with no network, and apps make it easy to stay in. Our class brief asked how young people experience loneliness in the age of AI. We focused on the first weeks after moving, when isolation sets in fastest and when a small, repeated ritual could make the biggest difference.

Our design principle. Technology should organise the meeting, not replace it. Cooking side by side gives people something to do with their hands, so the conversation happens naturally. The weekly table turns strangers into regulars.

My contribution · Building it

Version 0 to a live version 1

After the team work on the concept, we split the tasks and I took on the website. I built it from a clickable version 0 to a live version 1, deployed it on Vercel, and connected a Supabase database so we could collect real responses from ESADE students.

What students can do
A 6-step onboarding (university email, home country, what brings you here, cooking level) · table matching · dish picks from four countries · a weekly plan with a shopping list and swappable roles · the round · a group thread that stays open between weeks · XP and badges
What we learn from it
Feedback after every evening ("Did you feel more connected?", "Would you turn up next Wednesday?") and on the app itself, plus a private admin dashboard that exports every sign-up, dish choice and response to Excel or CSV

Privacy by design: responses shared at the table are opt-in, and the admin data needs a private key that is never in the public code.
