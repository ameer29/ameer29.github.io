01BBA summer internship project · 2020

Why do people stay with Netflix?

A study on the effect of consumer preference and attitude in online service marketing (B2C): Netflix

Amity Global Business School · BBA in MarketingGuide: Prof. Shanti JSurvey May – Jun 202017 questions92 responses

My first research project. During India's first COVID lockdown, streaming became everyone's evening, so I surveyed people on how they watch Netflix, what they value, and what would pull them to a rival.

81%

39%

What I found
  • A young, phone-first audience. 79% were students and 60% were aged 15–20. 41% watched mainly on a smartphone, 31% on a TV.
  • Content wins, price lags. "Many series" and "quality content" were the features most often rated satisfying. Pricing came last.
  • Long viewing sessions. 2–3 hours a day was the most common answer (31.5%), and 12% watched more than 5 hours.
  • Recommendations matter. 80% sometimes check Netflix's recommendation list before choosing something new, and 12.5% always do.
  • Tastes and plans. Thrillers (34%) and sci-fi/action (28%) were the favourite genres, and Premium was the most chosen plan (47%).

Method

An online questionnaire (Google Forms) shared through my network, covering usage (device, hours a day, tenure), satisfaction with five features, the service people would switch to, plan type, overall satisfaction and willingness to recommend. I analysed each question as a frequency table and chart and interpreted the percentages.

Looking back · my learning curve
  • A convenience sample of mostly students, so the results describe young viewers rather than all subscribers.
  • I'd now rate each feature on a 5-point scale instead of "tick if satisfied".
  • The analysis was descriptive. Since then I've learnt hypothesis testing, which I'd use to test relationships between answers.

02BBA dissertation · 2021

What keeps people ordering on Swiggy?

A study on the business model of Swiggy, Bangalore

Amity Global Business School · final-year dissertationGuide: Prof. S. KarthikeyanSurvey Mar – Apr 202120 questions93 responses

My final-year dissertation paired a business-model analysis of Swiggy with a consumer survey on how, how often and why people order food online.

84%

What I found
  • Who ordered. 83% were students and 54% were aged 15–20.
  • Habit, not occasion. Most people ordered once a week (48%) and at random (72%), not for weekends, guests or offers.
  • Discounts matter. 46% rated the impact of promotions 5 out of 5, and app discounts were the most-picked driver (65 respondents).
  • Quality comes first. Food quality was the top concern for 84%, ahead of speed and price.
  • Loyal users. 59% of subscribers had Swiggy Super, and 34% had used Swiggy for more than two years.

The business-model lens
  • Logistics at the core. Swiggy built its own delivery fleet and live tracking, making speed and reliability the product.
  • Fighting multi-homing. Customers compared Swiggy, Zomato and Uber Eats before ordering, so Swiggy signed exclusive deals and ranked those restaurants first.
  • Revenue. Restaurant commissions, delivery fees, the Swiggy Super subscription, and a move into groceries and other deliveries.
Looking back · my learning curve
  • Most respondents came through my network and already used Swiggy, so the satisfaction figures describe existing users.
  • The frequency question needed a "less than once a week" option.
  • The analysis was descriptive. Today I'd state hypotheses and test them, for example whether frequent users are more discount-driven.

03MSc field placement · 2026

Building trust through creative expression

Peer mentor field report · PEER Community Programme, Yayasan MENDAKI

MENDAKI Club · PEER @ TampinesSessions 1–4 · Feb – Apr 2026Written end of April 2026Observational field report

For the field placement in my MSc in Psychology, I documented how a group of secondary-school mentees (aged 11–16) went from guarded to open, and why play-based activities helped.

What I observed
14 Feb · OrientationMentees were reserved, as the mock rounds had predicted. Structured icebreakers and reflection journals gave them a low-pressure start.
28 Feb · Ramadan food distributionA turning point: in the debrief and their journals, mentees reflected on privilege and community.
14 Mar · Drawing & board games"Draw something that represents you" let them open up without direct questioning. Cooperative games replaced competition.
11 Apr · After the Hari Raya breakQuiet mentees were now starting conversations themselves. Trust was building step by step.

The report covers the first four sessions. I completed all 12, through 1 Aug 2026.

Framework: the 5Cs of Positive Youth Development
Every activity was mapped to these
CompetenceSocial, academic and life skills
ConfidenceSelf-worth and belief in the future
ConnectionBonds with mentors, peers, family
CharacterValues, empathy, integrity
ContributionGiving back to the community
Method
  • Participant observation as a mentor (2 mentors, 3 mentees)
  • Mentee reflection journals and group debriefs
  • Concepts from clinical psychology: rapport, group dynamics, prosocial development

04MSc dissertation · in progress

Leadership styles through the lens of Self-Determination Theory

The psychological impact of leadership styles on employee well-being and productivity: a Self-Determination Theory perspective

Jain University · MSc in PsychologyLiterature review draftedMethod and results: coming soon

Why do some managers bring out the best in people while others wear them down? Self-Determination Theory suggests one answer: people thrive when their needs for autonomy, competence and relatedness are met.

Research questions
  1. How do different leadership styles influence employee well-being and productivity?
  2. How do these styles support or frustrate the three basic psychological needs?
  3. Which approaches best sustain motivation, engagement and effectiveness over time?
Styles compared
TransformationalDemocraticSupportive & servantAuthoritarianLaissez-faireHybrid

Working proposition from the literature: need-supportive styles lead to higher well-being and more sustainable productivity, while controlling or absent leadership frustrates those needs. The study will test this.
